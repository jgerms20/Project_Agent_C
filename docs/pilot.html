Where Does the Pilot Start?

Four possible entry points into the story. Each creates a different tonal experience and relationship to the audience:

Opening Choice

The MAIP Notification

The moment the protagonist learns they're entering the industry. A notification that changes everything. Vulnerable, ambitious, raw beginnings.

Opening Choice

First Day at GSP

The surreal experience of starting a dream job on Zoom during Covid. Disorientation, high stakes, the allure of Goodby Silverstein & Partners colliding with pandemic reality.

Opening Choice

In Medias Res

Start at a later, more dramatic moment and flash back. Hook first, context second. Creates mystery and momentum from frame one.

Opening Choice

Before Advertising

The protagonist's life in the South, the decision to pursue this path. Context for ambition. Shows what they're leaving behind and what drives them forward.

What the Pilot Needs to Accomplish

  1. Introduce the protagonist and make us root for them. We need to understand their intelligence, their hunger, and the thing they're willing to risk.
  2. Establish the world of advertising — the allure AND the bullshit. Make it seductive. Make it dangerous. Show both.
  3. Set the tone — funny, sharp, with undercurrents of something darker. We're in smart-people territory, but the waters run deep and cold.
  4. Plant seeds for the season's themes — cognitive dissonance, performativeness, the gap between what people say and what they do. Subtle. Not on-the-nose.
  5. End with a hook — something that makes you need Episode 2. A moment, a revelation, a question that won't let go.
  6. Establish the show's unique voice and visual style. This is a world we've never quite seen on screen before, told by someone who actually lived it.

Pilot Tone

"This is going to be something special." The audience should feel like they're getting insider access to a world they've never seen, told by someone who actually lived it.

Tonal Elements

Smart & Insider

The dialogue crackles with intelligence. People understand subtext. There's a rhythm to how they speak that feels specific to this world—clever without trying.

Seductive

Advertising is beautiful. The work is gorgeous. The culture is intoxicating. We get why someone would give everything to be here. The trap is made of velvet.

Darkly Funny

Humor emerges from contradiction and irony, not from jokes. The funniest moments come from the gap between what people claim and what they do. Nothing is forced.

Ominous Undertow

Beneath the surface glamour, something is wrong. Not obvious. Not yet. But there's a current of discomfort, of inauthenticity, of systems that are beginning to crack. We sense it before we see it.
