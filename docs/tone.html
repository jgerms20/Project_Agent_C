How Tone Shifts by Episode

Like Atlanta seasons 2–3, individual episodes can radically shift register. The show has range. Not locked into one mode. The audience trusts that each episode will be its own thing.

Comedy

Absurd agency all-hands meetings. Corporate-speak theater. The small indignities played for maximum awkward laughs.

Drama

The layoff aftermath. The slow realization of what the agency actually is. Stakes that are real and personal.

Surreal

Entering the protagonist’s inner world. The gap between what’s said in a meeting and what it means. Dream-logic workplace.

Thriller-adjacent

Corporate espionage. Merger politics. Power moves made in hallways and calendar invites. The chess game made visible.

Genre Elements

The show operates across multiple genre registers simultaneously — the blend shifts episode to episode, but all six are always present in the background.

01

Drama

The core. Character-driven storytelling about real stakes — career, identity, belonging, and what it costs to be the only one in the room.

02

Comedy

Dark humor, absurdist corporate comedy, fish-out-of-water laughs. The joke is the gap between the rhetoric and the reality.

03

Romance

Workplace relationships, complications, the personal cost of professional ambition. Love as another arena where the same dynamics play out.

04

Thriller

Corporate politics, mergers, power moves, strategic chess. The tension of watching someone navigate a system designed to keep them out.

05

Crime

White-collar adjacent. Exploitative practices — of culture, of people, of creativity — framed as what they are: structural crime with no handcuffs.

06

Documentary Feel

Moments of authenticity that feel almost too real. The look, the language, the specificity of the world — earned by the creator having actually been there.

Visual Style TBD

Color Palette by Era

Each agency era has its own visual language — grounded in the city, the office aesthetic, and how the protagonist experiences that world.

Era 1 — GSP / San Francisco

Cool, blue-tinted, screen-lit. The digital remove of remote work. Everything experienced through glass.

Era 2 — W+K / Portland

Warm, saturated, physical. The shock of being in a body in an office. Rich greens, textured surfaces, rain.

Era 3 — TBWA / Los Angeles

Golden, hazy, LA-ambient. Post-everything ease on the surface. The light that makes everything look fine.
