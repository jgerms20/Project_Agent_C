Themes

The Agency operates on multiple thematic layers simultaneously. Themes flex in prominence by episode and season.
Core Themes
01

Cognitive Dissonance

What it means in the show

The gap between what agencies say and what they do. "We value diversity" while the only Black junior strategist never gets promoted. "We're a family" while laying people off by Zoom.

How it manifests

Every agency era has its own version. GSP is performative diversity. W+K is cult mythology vs. corporate reality. TBWA is "disruption" philosophy vs. merger machine.

Key question
How long can you participate in a system you know is lying?
02

Cult-Like Buy-In (Or Opt Out)

What it means in the show

Agencies don't just want employees; they want believers. Total buy-in — identity, time, creativity, loyalty. The alternative is exile.

How it manifests

Most visible at W+K. "Just Do It" isn't just a tagline; it's a way of life. But what happens when the cult doesn't want you anymore?

Key question
What does it cost to belong? What does it cost to refuse?
03

A Looming Apocalypse

What it means in the show

The advertising industry as we know it is ending. AI, consolidation, cultural shifts. The old guard doesn't see it coming.

How it manifests

AI as slow-burn Easter eggs leading to a crescendo. Merger rumblings. Layoffs. Something massive coming but nobody talking about it.

Key question
When you see the end coming, do you run, hide, or build?
04

Cultural + Societal Shifts

What it means in the show

2020s unprecedented upheaval — pandemic, BLM, polarization, generational conflict, remote work revolution. The ad industry claims to reflect culture but is always behind it.

How it manifests

Each season is marked by real cultural moments. The show doesn't lecture — it shows how they ripple through workplaces and relationships.

Key question
Can an industry that sells culture actually understand it?
05

Reclamation

What it means in the show

Taking back what's been co-opted. Black culture, Southern identity, creative vision, personal agency.

How it manifests

The protagonist's arc from being the "diverse hire" to changing the game on their own terms.

Key question
How do you take back what was stolen without becoming what stole it?
06

First-Mover's Advantage

What it means in the show

Who sees the future first? The protagonist's strategic mind — the ability to see patterns before others do.

How it manifests

Chess/Go metaphors. A growing ability to read the room. AI as the ultimate first-mover test.

Key question
What's the advantage worth if nobody listens until it's too late?
07

Performativeness

What it means in the show

Theater of progressive values in corporate spaces. DEI as PR. Allyship that evaporates when inconvenient. An industry that runs entirely on performance.

How it manifests

GSP during BLM. W+K's "creative culture." TBWA's "disruption." Every agency performs a version of itself for the world.

Key question
In an industry built on selling images, is anything real?
08

Allyship vs. Alliance

What it means in the show

The spectrum from fake solidarity to real partnership. Some perform allyship; others actually show up when it counts.

How it manifests

The white creative who posts a black square but stays silent in the room. The unexpected ally who risks something real. The fellow POC who has been co-opted.

Key question
How do you know who's really with you?
09

Creativity vs. Capitalism

What it means in the show

The soul of advertising. Art or commerce? Can it be both? What gets sacrificed when the answer turns out to be no?

How it manifests

Great work killed for business reasons. Derivative work celebrated. The constant tension between what's meaningful and what's profitable.

Key question
Can you make art inside a machine built to make money?
10

The Long Con

What it means in the show

Multiple levels. The industry's con on its workers. The protagonist's long game of learning the system in order to disrupt it. The show itself as a long con.

How it manifests

Seeds planted in early episodes pay off seasons later. The protagonist's moves are only fully visible in retrospect.

Key question
Who's conning whom?
