# Season Arcs

## Series Structure

4-5+ seasons planned. Each season broadly covers one agency era, with non-linear jumps (flashbacks, flash-forwards, blended timelines).

---

## Season 1 — "The Awakening" (Goodby Silverstein & Partners, San Francisco)
**Timeline**: 2020-2021 (pandemic onset through first year)
**Episodes**: TBD (8-10 target for half-hour)

### Season Arc
The protagonist enters the advertising industry through MAIP at the worst/best possible moment — right as Covid hits and the world breaks open. Everything is remote. They've "made it" to one of the most prestigious agencies in the country, but they experience it through a screen. They watch the industry perform progressive values during BLM while doing nothing to change. They're the junior strategist who sees everything but can say nothing.

### Key Beats (To Be Developed)
- [ ] The MAIP entry / getting the notification
- [ ] First day at GSP (remote — the absurdity of starting a dream job on Zoom)
- [ ] George Floyd / BLM — watching the agency's response in real time
- [ ] The vaccination incident (denied office access)
- [ ] Being junior strategist and never getting promoted
- [ ] Relationships formed remotely — the MAIP cohort as lifeline
- [ ] Discovering the gap between agency mythology and reality
- [ ] Season finale — what pushes them toward leaving?

### Dominant Themes
Cognitive Dissonance, Performativeness, Cultural Shifts, Allyship vs. Alliance

### Tone
Observational. Funny in the way that absurd corporate behavior is funny. Lonely in the way that remote work during a pandemic is lonely. The protagonist is watching, learning, and slowly realizing what they've entered.

## Season 2 — "The Cult" (Wieden+Kennedy, Portland)
**Timeline**: 2023-2024
**Episodes**: TBD

### Season Arc
The protagonist moves to Portland and enters the cult. For the first time, they're physically inside an agency — and it's everything they imagined and nothing like they expected. W+K's mythology is intoxicating. The work is legendary. The buy-in is total. The protagonist gets swept up, achieves, belongs... and then gets laid off. The cult doesn't want them anymore.

### Key Beats (To Be Developed)
- [ ] Arriving in Portland — fish out of water (Southern Black person in the whitest city)
- [ ] First day in-person at an agency — sensory overload after years of Zoom
- [ ] The W+K mythology — the pitch, the history, the Nike of it all
- [ ] Buying in — the high of belonging
- [ ] "Keep Portland Weird" — navigating Portland's particular brand of progressive weirdness
- [ ] The work — what they created, what mattered, what got killed
- [ ] Cracks in the cult — seeing what's behind the curtain
- [ ] The layoff — how it happened, what it felt like, the aftermath
- [ ] Season finale — leaving Portland, heading to LA, changed

### Dominant Themes
Cult-Like Buy-In, Creativity vs. Capitalism, Reclamation, The Long Con

### Tone
More intense. Higher highs and lower lows. The comedy comes from the cult absurdity; the drama comes from genuine emotional investment that gets betrayed. The layoff episode should be devastating.

## Season 3 — "The Reckoning" (TBWA\Chiat\Day, Los Angeles)
**Timeline**: 2024-2025
**Episodes**: TBD

### Season Arc
The protagonist arrives in LA with scars and clarity. TBWA is chill, corporate, part of the Omnicom machine. The cult energy is gone — replaced by ambient corporate entropy. But the protagonist has changed. They see the full picture now: the industry, AI, the consolidation, the long con. This season is about strategic positioning — the protagonist begins making moves.

### Key Beats (To Be Developed)
- [ ] Landing in LA — the energy shift from Portland
- [ ] TBWA culture — "Disruption" philosophy meets corporate reality
- [ ] The Omnicom merger — rumblings, implications, what it means for everyone
- [ ] AI becoming impossible to ignore — tools in meetings, people losing jobs
- [ ] The protagonist's strategic awakening — using AI, not fearing it
- [ ] Building real alliances (vs. the performative ones of earlier seasons)
- [ ] The catalyst moment — when the protagonist decides to disrupt

### Dominant Themes
Looming Apocalypse, First-Mover's Advantage, The Long Con, Reclamation

### Tone
Calmer surface, deeper currents. The comedy is drier. The drama is more strategic than emotional. The protagonist is no longer reactive — they're playing the game.

## Non-Linear Elements

The show uses non-linear storytelling within the season-per-era structure:

- **Flashbacks**: Protagonist's life before advertising (the South, college, the decision to enter the industry)
- **Flash-forwards**: Brief glimpses of where things are heading (creates dramatic irony)
- **Blended timelines**: Episodes that cut between eras to draw thematic parallels
- **MAIP threads**: The MAIP cohort provides continuity across all eras — characters who appear in every season regardless of agency
