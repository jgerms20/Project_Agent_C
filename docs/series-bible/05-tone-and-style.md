# Tone & Style

## Tonal DNA

**Primary**: Drama-forward with dark humor
**Secondary**: Episode-dependent tonal shifts — the show earns the right to be funny, devastating, and surreal in equal measure

### The Spectrum
- **Light ↔ Dark**: Leans dark (3 out of 5)
- **Simple ↔ Complex**: Leans complex
- **Casual ↔ Formal**: Casual-leaning
- **Niche ↔ Broad**: Slightly niche — IYKYK energy that still tells a universal story

### How Tone Shifts by Episode
Like Atlanta seasons 2-3, individual episodes can radically shift tone:
- A **comedy episode** might follow a day of absurd agency all-hands meetings
- A **dramatic episode** might explore the layoff and its aftermath
- A **surreal episode** might enter the protagonist's inner world or use metaphor to explore a theme
- A **thriller-adjacent episode** might follow corporate espionage or merger politics

The key: the show has *range*. It's not locked into one mode. The audience learns to trust that each episode will be its own thing while serving the larger story.

## Genre Elements

The show pulls from multiple genres:
- **Drama**: The core. Character-driven storytelling about real stakes.
- **Comedy**: Dark humor, absurdist corporate comedy, fish-out-of-water laughs
- **Romance**: Workplace relationships, complications, the personal cost of ambition
- **Thriller**: Corporate politics, mergers, power moves, strategic chess
- **Crime**: White-collar adjacent — the industry's exploitative practices framed as structural crime
- **Documentary feel**: Moments of authenticity that feel almost too real (inspired by real events)
