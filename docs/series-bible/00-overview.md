A young Black strategist from the South navigates the elite, contradictory world of America's most prestigious advertising agencies during the most turbulent era the industry has ever faced — from pandemic performativeness to AI apocalypse — transforming from wide-eyed outsider to the one who disrupts it all.

---

## Premise

The disruption of the advertising industry, post-2020, from the outside in.

Three agencies. Three cities. One protagonist who enters advertising at the exact moment the industry begins eating itself — and slowly learns to use that chaos as a weapon.

The show follows our protagonist through three distinct agency eras (San Francisco → Portland → Los Angeles), each representing a different face of the same broken system. What begins as a fish-out-of-water story about a Southern kid in a sophisticated coastal industry becomes something much larger: a story about who gets to tell the stories that shape culture, who profits from them, and what happens when the old guard's con finally runs out of road.

## Comparable Shows

### Mad Men
**What we share**: Advertising setting, character depth and breadth, sweeping industry and cultural changes, period piece quality, the intersection of work and life
**What we do differently**: Contemporary. Diverse perspective at the center. The industry is declining, not ascending.

### Atlanta (S2-S3 especially)
**What we share**: Half-hour format, tonal range (funny then devastating), standalone episodes that build something bigger, Black perspective navigating predominantly white/wealthy spaces, surreal touches
**What we do differently**: Workplace-centered. Industry-specific insider knowledge. More serialized through-lines.

### Silicon Valley
**What we share**: Comedic tone grounded in very real experiences, fake company surrounded by real industry dynamics, deep insider knowledge, IYKYK humor
**What we do differently**: More dramatic weight. Race and culture are central, not peripheral. Higher emotional stakes.

### Industry
**What we share**: Serious and salacious tone, underdog-to-power-player arc, cutthroat nature explored honestly, changing of the guard in real time, killer soundtrack
**What we do differently**: American. Advertising not finance. More comedic range. Southern perspective.

## Format & Structure

- **Episode length**: ~30 minutes
- **Episode style**: Hybrid — episodes can stand alone thematically while building season-long arcs
- **Tonal range**: Some episodes lean comedic, some lean heavy/dramatic, some are surreal or experimental (Atlanta-style flex)
- **Timeline**: Each season broadly covers one agency era, with non-linear jumps (flashbacks, flash-forwards, blended timelines)
- **POV**: Primarily the protagonist, but ensemble cast with multiple POV episodes

---

## The AI Thread

AI functions as a slow-burn undercurrent throughout the entire series:

- **Early seasons**: Virtually invisible. Hidden Easter eggs — someone leaves to start a company, an unexplained layoff, a quick mention in a meeting. Planted so that on rewatch, it's obvious.
- **Middle seasons**: ChatGPT arrives (Nov 2022 timeline). Characters start using it. Still feels like a curiosity, not a threat.
- **Later seasons**: AI reaches ~80% intensity. Omnicom merger rumblings. Industry consolidation. The old way of doing things is visibly dying.
- **Climax**: The protagonist uses AI as a catalyst — not just surviving the disruption, but weaponizing it.
