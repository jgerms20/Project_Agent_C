# Comparable Shows Analysis

## Primary Comps

### Mad Men (AMC, 2007-2015)
**Creator**: Matthew Weiner
**Format**: Hour-long drama, 7 seasons
**Setting**: 1960s New York advertising (Sterling Cooper)

**What We Share**:
- Advertising as the lens for exploring bigger cultural questions
- Character depth and breadth — ensemble cast with rich inner lives
- Sweeping changes in the industry mirroring sweeping changes in culture
- Period piece quality — our show is a period piece of the 2020s
- The intersection of work and life — the job IS the identity
- A protagonist who is both insider and outsider

**What We Do Differently**:
- Contemporary, not nostalgic. The industry is declining, not ascending.
- Diverse perspective at the center, not the periphery
- Half-hour hybrid, not hour-long drama
- More comedic range
- Technology (AI) as existential threat, not just creative tool

**Lessons to Learn**:
- How Mad Men used real brands and real cultural events to ground fiction
- The slow-burn character development across seasons
- How it made the ad industry feel important and fascinating
- Don Draper's outsider-who-passes as the ur-archetype for our protagonist

---

### Atlanta (FX, 2016-2022)
**Creator**: Donald Glover
**Format**: Half-hour, 4 seasons
**Setting**: Atlanta's rap/music scene and beyond

**What We Share**:
- Half-hour format with tonal range (funny then devastating)
- Standalone episodes that build something bigger
- Black perspective navigating spaces not built for them
- Surreal touches grounded in real experience
- Episode-dependent tone shifts — some comedic, some haunting
- The feeling of being an outsider in an industry that commodifies your culture

**What We Do Differently**:
- Workplace-centered (advertising, not music)
- More industry-specific insider knowledge
- More serialized through-lines
- More directly autobiographical
- Corporate spaces, not street-level spaces

**Lessons to Learn**:
- How Atlanta uses individual episodes as standalone short films
- The surreal episodes (Teddy Perkins, the European tour) as creative freedom
- How it handles race without making every episode "about race"
- The confidence to let episodes breathe and be weird

---

### Silicon Valley (HBO, 2014-2019)
**Creator**: Mike Judge
**Format**: Half-hour comedy, 6 seasons
**Setting**: Silicon Valley tech startup world

**What We Share**:
- Comedic tone grounded in very real industry experiences
- Fake company and storyline surrounded by real industry dynamics
- Deep insider knowledge — IYKYK humor
- The absurdity of corporate culture taken seriously
- An outsider protagonist navigating elite spaces

**What We Do Differently**:
- More dramatic weight and emotional depth
- Race and culture are central, not peripheral
- Higher emotional stakes (it's not just about the product; it's about identity)
- Three distinct workplace settings, not one company
- Less broad comedy, more dark humor

**Lessons to Learn**:
- How Silicon Valley made tech accessible while keeping the insider details
- The balance of comedy and real industry critique
- Using real companies and real dynamics as background texture
- The "fake company, real world" model

---

### Industry (HBO, 2020-present)
**Creator**: Mickey Down, Konrad Kay
**Format**: Hour-long drama
**Setting**: London investment banking

**What We Share**:
- Serious and salacious tone
- Underdog-to-power-player arc
- Cutthroat corporate nature explored honestly
- Changing of the guard in real time
- Killer soundtrack / music integration
- Young diverse cast navigating old-money institutions
- The seduction and destruction of corporate buy-in

**What We Do Differently**:
- American, not British
- Advertising, not finance
- More comedic range
- Southern perspective as the outsider lens
- Less sexual, more culturally complex
- AI as a disruptive force (finance hasn't faced this the same way)

**Lessons to Learn**:
- How Industry uses music as a character
- The raw depiction of workplace power dynamics
- Season 3's expansion of scope as a model for how our show could grow
- The authenticity of depicting an industry the creators actually worked in
