Series Bible

The complete creative document for The Agency / Agency

Overview

Title: The Agency / Agency

Format: Half-Hour Hybrid (standalone episodes building a larger arc)

Seasons: 4-5+ planned

Tone: Drama-forward with dark humor; tonal shifts by episode

Logline

DRAFT

A young Black strategist from the South navigates the elite, contradictory world of America's most prestigious advertising agencies during the most turbulent era the industry has ever faced — from pandemic performativeness to AI apocalypse — transforming from wide-eyed outsider to the one who disrupts it all.

Premise

The disruption of the advertising industry, post-2020, from the outside in.

Three agencies. Three cities. One protagonist who enters advertising at the exact moment the industry begins eating itself — and slowly learns to use that chaos as a weapon.

The show follows our protagonist through three distinct agency eras (San Francisco → Portland → Los Angeles), each representing a different face of the same broken system. What begins as a fish-out-of-water story about a Southern kid in a sophisticated coastal industry becomes something much larger: a story about who gets to tell the stories that shape culture, who profits from them, and what happens when the old guard's con finally runs out of road.

Format & Structure

  • Episode length: ~30 minutes
  • Episode style: Hybrid — standalone thematically while building season arcs
  • Tonal range: Comedic, dramatic, surreal, experimental (Atlanta-style flex)
  • Timeline: Season per era, with non-linear jumps
  • POV: Primarily protagonist, ensemble with multiple POV episodes

The AI Thread

AI functions as a slow-burn undercurrent throughout the entire series:

  • Early seasons: Hidden Easter eggs — someone leaves to start a company, an unexplained layoff. On rewatch, it's obvious.
  • Middle seasons: ChatGPT arrives (Nov 2022). Characters start using it. Still a curiosity.
  • Later seasons: AI at ~80% intensity. Omnicom merger. Industry consolidation. The old way is dying.
  • Climax: The protagonist uses AI as a catalyst — weaponizing the disruption.

The Advertising Industry, 2020-2026

The show is set during the most turbulent period in advertising since the Mad Men era:

  • Reckoning with diversity — post-George Floyd, agencies scramble to prove they're not what they've always been
  • Surviving a pandemic — remote work reshapes agency culture overnight
  • Facing an AI apocalypse — tools that were supposed to help creatives start replacing them
  • Consolidating — mergers swallow agencies whole
  • Losing its soul — or maybe admitting it never had one

This is a show about an industry in decline that doesn't know it's declining — told by someone watching it happen from the inside.

The Three Worlds

San Francisco / Goodby Silverstein & Partners (2020-2023)

"The Awakening"

ERA 1

The Agency

One of the most respected independent agencies. "Got Milk?" legacy. Academic energy — these people think they're the smartest in the room, and they might be. But smart doesn't mean good.

The City

San Francisco during/post-Covid. The protagonist experiences it entirely remotely — the city exists as a Zoom background, a mythology they're not part of.

The Culture

Peak performativeness. Diversity statements flying while diverse people get sidelined. The protagonist enters eyes wide open and watches the contradictions pile up.

The Mood

Isolation, observation, slow awakening, cognitive dissonance

Portland / Wieden+Kennedy (2023-2024)

"The Cult"

ERA 2

The Agency

The most famous advertising agency in the world. Nike's agency. "Just Do It." The mythology is intoxicating — and designed to be. Buy in or get out.

The City

Portland, Oregon. "Keep Portland Weird." The protagonist — a Black Southerner — navigating this aggressively "open-minded" but very white city.

The Culture

Cult-like buy-in at its peak. Corporate culture masquerading as creative revolution. First time in-person — the protagonist finally experiences what agency life feels like.

The Mood

Intensity, belonging and alienation simultaneously, creative highs, the axe waiting to fall

Los Angeles / TBWA\Chiat\Day (2024-present)

"The Reckoning"

ERA 3

The Agency

Part of the Omnicom machine. Chill energy masking corporate machinery. "Disruption" philosophy — ironic given the un-disruptive day-to-day.

The City

Los Angeles. Everyone is working on something. Less intense office culture, more ambient ambition.

The Culture

Post-everything. AI is in the room. The protagonist understands the game — the question is what to do with that knowledge.

The Mood

Clarity, strategy, accumulation, the calm before the move

Core Themes

The Agency operates on multiple thematic layers simultaneously. Themes flex in prominence by episode and season.

1. Cognitive Dissonance

The gap between what agencies say and do. How long can you participate in a system you know is lying?

2. Cult-Like Buy-In

Agencies want believers, not employees. Total buy-in or exile. What does it cost to belong? To refuse?

3. A Looming Apocalypse

AI, consolidation, cultural shifts converging. When you see the end coming, do you run, hide, or build?

4. Cultural + Societal Shifts

Pandemic, BLM, political polarization rippling through workplaces. Can an industry that sells culture understand it?

5. Reclamation

Taking back what's been co-opted — Black culture, Southern identity, creative vision. How do you take back what was stolen without becoming what stole it?

6. First-Mover's Advantage

Who sees the future first? Strategic Go metaphors. What's the advantage worth if nobody listens?

7. Performativeness

DEI theater. Allyship that evaporates when inconvenient. In an industry built on selling images, is anything real?

8. Allyship vs. Alliance

The spectrum from fake solidarity to real partnership. How do you know who's really with you?

9. Creativity vs. Capitalism

Great work killed for business. Derivative work celebrated. Can you make art inside a machine built to make money?

10. The Long Con

The industry's con on workers. The protagonist's con on the industry. Who's conning whom?

Season 1 — "The Awakening"

GSP • San Francisco • 2020-2021

Episodes: TBD (8-10 target)

Arc

The protagonist enters through MAIP as Covid hits. Everything is remote. They watch the industry perform progressive values during BLM while doing nothing. The junior strategist who sees everything but can say nothing.

Key Beats

  • The MAIP entry / getting the notification
  • First day at GSP (starting a dream job on Zoom)
  • George Floyd / BLM — watching the agency's response
  • The vaccination incident (denied office access)
  • Junior strategist, never promoted
  • MAIP cohort as lifeline
  • Discovering the gap between mythology and reality
  • Season finale — what pushes them to leave?

Themes

Cognitive Dissonance Performativeness Cultural Shifts Allyship

Season 2 — "The Cult"

W+K • Portland • 2023-2024

Arc

The protagonist enters the cult. First time physically inside an agency. W+K's mythology is intoxicating. Gets swept up, achieves, belongs... then gets laid off. The cult doesn't want them anymore.

Key Beats

  • Arriving in Portland — fish out of water
  • First day in-person — sensory overload after Zoom years
  • The W+K mythology — Nike, history, the pitch
  • Buying in — the high of belonging
  • "Keep Portland Weird" — navigating Portland
  • The work — what was created, killed
  • Cracks in the cult
  • The layoff
  • Season finale — leaving Portland, changed

Cult Buy-In Creativity vs. Capitalism Reclamation The Long Con
